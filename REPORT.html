D1 — Educational Problem Statement

In Hindi-medium schools across eastern Uttar Pradesh and Bihar, learners with a primary Bhojpuri linguistic background encounter pervasive orthographic and phonological interference when acquiring standard written Hindi (खड़ी बोली). In Bhojpuri phonological grammar, the palatal fricative /ʃ/ (श) naturally neutralizes into the dental fricative /s/ (स), creating acoustic homophony. In addition, the retroflex flap /ɽ/ (ड़), indicated by the diacritical nukta, is habitually replaced by the un-nukta stop [ɖ] (ड).

Consequently, students routinely substitute words of fundamentally distinct meanings (e.g. शेर [lion] vs सेर [measurement]) and omit nuktas in common academic words (सड़क, लड़का). Conventional educational tools either penalize regional dialectal features as illiterate errors or fail to provide auditory contrast. This application bridges that gap through situated contrastive analysis, auditory speech modeling, and asset-based AI conversational practice.

Measurable Learning Outcomes (Action-Verb Aligned):

  1. Identify (पहचानना): Given visual cues or speech prompts, the learner will correctly identify whether standard Hindi requires palatal श or dental स with at least 85% accuracy.
  2. Distinguish (अंतर करना): The learner will distinguish acceptable oral Bhojpuri dialect forms (e.g. सादी, सरम) from written orthographic errors, explaining why formal written Hindi necessitates standard spelling.
  3. Apply (प्रयोग करना): In contextual sentence completion and communicative dialogue with an AI tutor, the learner will apply the nukta rule for ड़ and correct sibilants श/स in everyday vocabulary.

D3 — Educational Content Plan & Learning Tasks

The instructional sequence builds systematically from perceptual discrimination to production, structured around Sweller's Cognitive Load Theory and Bloom's Taxonomy:

Structured Curriculum Progression

  • Lesson 1 (श vs स): Articulatory vocal tract differentiation (palatal vs dental) with clean spoken audio and interactive anatomical diagrams.
  • Lesson 2 (ड़ vs ड): Stop plosive vs flap distinction and the position rule of the nukta (never at the beginning of a word).
  • Lesson 3 (वाक्य प्रयोग): Real-world sentence construction, contrastive pairs, and sociolinguistic validation of spoken dialect vs written standard Hindi.

Key Built-in Pedagogical Features:

  • On-Screen Hindi Devanagari Keyboard (<HindiKeyboard />): Solves the barrier of rural learners lacking native Devanagari input on mobile web browsers, providing one-tap access to target letters (श, स, ड़, ड) and common words.
  • Interactive Articulation Visualizer (<PhoneticsVisualizer />): Dynamic visual model demonstrating vocal tract placement (palatal vs dental, stop vs flap) accompanied by authentic TTS audio pronunciation.
  • Speech Normalization Engine (cleanTextForSpeech): Strips markdown, emojis, dashes, and bullets before audio generation, ensuring natural spoken Hindi without literal emoji recitation.
  • Per-User Session Isolation & Logout: Dedicated session keys (progress_<userId>) ensure each student's progress and test scores remain completely private and isolated.

D7 — Pedagogical Rationale & Design Challenge Resolution

Resolution of P2 Design Challenge

"How will the application distinguish a learner's language errors from acceptable dialect or pronunciation variations? Show one example of each in your app."

  • Theoretical Framework: Grounded in Labov's sociolinguistic constructivism, dialectal phonology represents legitimate rule-governed linguistic variation rather than cognitive deficiency.
  • Dialect Variation (Validated): When the student writes "सादी" or "सरम", the app classifies this under phonological transfer from Bhojpuri. The system validates the student's communicative intent: "यह आपकी बोली की विशेषता है, गलत नहीं है!" while bridging to formal written standard Hindi.
  • Language Error (Corrective Feedback): When the student writes "सक्ति" for power or "सडक" without nukta, the app classifies this as an orthographic error (✏️ वर्तनी सुधार) requiring phonemic and articulatory correction.
